# References and provenance

GCMSEM is implemented independently of MCMSEM; it has no MCMSEM runtime dependency. Its statistical motivation comes from higher-moment structural equation modeling and LD-score regression. The initial Python algebra was developed and checked in the preceding LD-moment pilot, then packaged and extended here.

* Bulik-Sullivan et al. (2015). LD Score regression distinguishes confounding from polygenicity in genome-wide association studies. Nature Genetics. DOI: [10.1038/ng.3211](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3211).
* Bulik-Sullivan et al. (2015). An atlas of genetic correlations across human diseases and traits. Nature Genetics. DOI: [10.1038/ng.3406](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3406).
* Grotzinger et al. (2019). Genomic structural equation modelling provides insights into the multivariate genetic architecture of complex traits. Nature Human Behaviour. DOI: [10.1038/s41562-019-0566-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0566-x). See [GenomicSEM source](https://github.com/GenomicSEM/GenomicSEM) for the diagonal fitting weights/full-covariance sandwich approach.
* [lavaan model syntax](https://lavaan.ugent.be/tutorial/syntax1.html) and [parameter modifiers](https://lavaan.ugent.be/tutorial/syntax2.html) inform the syntax. GCMSEM implements its own parser and does not claim full lavaan compatibility.
* [GWASBrewer documentation](https://jean997.github.io/GWASBrewer/) and [source](https://github.com/jean997/GWASBrewer) describe the simulator and bundled HapMap3 chromosome 19 LD pattern. GWASBrewer's effect matrix uses from-trait rows and to-trait columns; GCMSEM's internal regression matrix uses outcome rows and predictor columns.

Reference LD and GWAS inputs retain their original licenses. They are supplied by the user or generated by the validation scripts; this repository does not redistribute the empirical input archives from the pilot.
